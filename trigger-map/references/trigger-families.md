# Trigger families

All 34 event families, plus the 7 buying states that no topic catalog names. Use the event words to
recognise a family in a quote, a headline or a job post. "Buyers said it came first" counts the markets
(of 24 researched) where a real buyer, in public, said this event came before they bought. Families with
no count are real events but have no buyer proof yet: treat them as a message angle, not a reason to call.

## Part A: buying states (check these first, they carry the most proof)

| State | What it looks like | Cheapest place to see it | Buyers said it came first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Growth outgrows the tool** | volume doubles; "the spreadsheet that worked at half the size"; hiring many people for one manual job | job posts (several openings for the same ops role), the company's own blog or press | 8 markets |
| **Incumbent vendor disruption** | the current vendor is acquired, raises prices, sunsets a product, has outages | the vendor's end-of-life or pricing page, review sites, community forums | 7 markets |
| **Fragmented or manual systems** | "five tools that don't talk", copy-paste between systems, paper processes | job posts ("reconcile data across", "manual"), reviews written by staff | 4 markets |
| **Regulation takes effect** | a new rule with an effective date that applies to every company in the segment | the regulator's own site, industry press | 3 markets |
| **Contract or renewal coming up** | public tender, RFP, contract end date, "replacing X after our contract ends" | public procurement portals (public sector), job posts naming the incumbent tool | 5 markets |
| **Key hire with a buying mandate** | a new role whose post says "evaluate, select and implement" a tool | ATS job boards (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable) | 1 of 7 markets confirmed |
| **New leader** | a new exec in the seat that owns the budget, first 90 days | company press, LinkedIn announcements (company page) | 1 market |

## How to read the proof column

- Strongest across markets: M&A (10), expansion (8), growth outgrows the tool (8), vendor disruption (7),
  audit or inspection (7), renewal or RFP (5), migration (5).
- Generic company events (funding, a launch, a headline) happen to about half of all companies within 9
  months. They make good message openers but don't separate buyers from everyone else. Pick accounts by
  fit, then use the event to time and word the message.
- Layoffs, restructuring and bankruptcy mostly pause buying. Read them as "wait".
